# Experimental input sync

Open two or more containers of the same app in virtual windows and place them side by side. In the Dock's layout menu (or Multitasking settings), open **Input Sync (Experimental)**. Choose the source, followers and mode, align all app screens, then start. Changing the source within the same app keeps the other selected accounts and makes the previous source a follower when replay is available.

The last source, followers and mode are saved automatically by container identifier. Relaunching restores the selection paused; accounts opened later remain selected, even when the window order changes. Source-only choices and clearing the group are also remembered. Restoring a workspace replaces this last selection with its saved group. Floating-control size, role labels and portrait/landscape positions keep their existing preferences. Forwarding never starts automatically after relaunch or restore.

Setup has four remembered **Automatic Resume** options, all off by default:

- **Resume When Returning to Source**: operating any selected follower pauses the whole group and cancels the input currently being replayed. Returning means beginning a new direct touch in the source app. That first gesture stays local; after all fingers on every participant are lifted and the windows settle, subsequent source gestures are synchronized automatically. Finishing a follower gesture alone does not resume. A later follower gesture requires another return to the source.
- **Sync the First Returning Gesture**: available when Resume When Returning to Source is enabled. A new source touch starts preparation immediately if the group's geometry is unchanged and every follower has acknowledged cancellation and released its physical fingers. The returning gesture is captured briefly while followers prepare, then its original down/moves/up are synchronized; a held source contact continues into the new session. The first touch still reaches the source normally. Another held follower delays recovery, and an expired capture or a changed window cannot be replayed. With this option off, the first return gesture keeps the local behavior above.
- **Resume After Screen Rotation**: an actual interface rotation pauses and cancels the current input, then resumes after all fingers are lifted and the windows settle. Ordinary window resizing, grid edits, minimizing, PiP and closing windows still require an explicit resume. If a follower is operated during rotation with both options on, the group waits for a return to the source.
- **Resume When Returning to Foreground**: only a group actively synchronizing immediately before LiveContainer entered the background can resume. Backgrounding cancels all synthetic contacts and discards queued input. Returning from Home, the App Switcher or lock starts a fresh session after the host scene is active, the visible layout settles and every participant passes a new preparation check. No extra source touch is needed. Manual Pause, setup, group/mode/option edits, interruption failures, disconnection, and relaunch or workspace restoration cancel this intent. A group already waiting for follower/rotation recovery or still starting does not resume. Temporary inactivity from Control Center or a system prompt without entering the background stays paused. Application and host-scene background/foreground notifications are handled together; an interrupted return keeps the same pending intent until it resumes or is explicitly cancelled.

These options apply only to the current running session. Manual Pause, opening setup, changing the group or mode, showing the keyboard or a blocking dialog, and disconnecting cancel automatic recovery. Leaving the foreground cancels source/rotation recovery; the separate foreground option can retain only the intent of a group that was actively running. The floating control shows the waiting reason while recovering; tapping its Pause button keeps the group paused. Fresh input checks must succeed before automatic forwarding resumes. Only the explicitly enabled new returning gesture can be handed over; interrupted input and expired captures are discarded. Preparation is asynchronous, so this option removes the extra touch without guaranteeing zero delay.

- **Taps Only** (default): repeat completed direct taps lasting at most 350 ms and moving at most 10 guest points. Swipes and long presses remain local, matching the original tap-sync behavior.
- **Single Finger**: forward began, moved, ended and cancelled phases while keeping one contact down. Supports taps, swipes, scrolling, continuous dragging and stationary long presses. A second finger pauses and cancels the group. Pinch/rotation, simultaneous joystick and button touches, system gestures, pointers and keyboard replay are outside this mode.
- **Two Fingers**: forward up to two simultaneous direct contacts in atomic frames, including stationary fingers. Intended for pinch/rotation and holding a joystick while tapping another control. Releasing one finger keeps the other down. A third active finger pauses and cancels the group. System gestures, pointers and keyboard input remain excluded. This mode requires app-specific device testing.

The floating control consistently toggles pause/resume; the separate gear opens setup and pauses forwarding. Drag either part to dock the control at the left or right edge. Portrait and landscape positions are saved separately and clamped to the safe area. Setup includes compact controls, optional numbered source/follower labels and a position reset. VoiceOver offers corner-placement actions. Labels do not intercept app touches, and controls stay available when the Dock is hidden. A paused expanded control shows its reason; setup shows the full status. Clearing the group removes the controls.

Failed starts and unexpected interruptions also show their reason in compact controls, with a warning icon. The gear opens full, separate reasons for every affected window. Startup collects all follower preparation results before proceeding to the source, so several unavailable followers are reported together; a timed-out check does not erase other windows' specific failures. Foreground inactivity, a covering window/dialog, an unready input window, an open keyboard, a physical touch, a check timeout and an unavailable input connection have separate messages.

Window title menus and Dock app menus offer **Use as Sync Source** and **Remove from Sync Group**. Source changes within the same app preserve other selected followers, including temporarily closed accounts, keep their order and make the previous source a follower when replay is available. Promoting a follower also preserves the remaining members when the old source has closed. Changes always pause before taking effect. When a participant interrupts, disconnects, changes its layout or fails to reply, setup identifies the affected window(s) and offers removal of followers. Timeout attribution lists only participants whose reply is still outstanding. Unknown/global failures are not attributed to an arbitrary window.

Window bars, grid dividers and host controls are outside the guest capture path. Coordinates are relative to the guest window, independent of the host's reserved margins. Followers must have the same interface orientation and an aspect ratio within 2.5%; content scales can differ, although matching sizes/scales help prevent app layout differences. After beginning inside the guest, a finger may track outside its initial bounds; its position is not clamped onto an edge button. Moving more than one window dimension beyond an edge interrupts the gesture.

Input sync reproduces input, not app state. Loading screens, dialogs, animations and account-specific screens can diverge. Delivery is sequential and asynchronous, not frame-locked. The count increases after a tap or an ended gesture reaches every selected guest input pipeline; move packets and cancellations do not inflate it. It does not confirm that apps performed the intended action. Game and SwiftUI compatibility needs device testing.

Changing geometry, minimizing/closing windows, PiP, leaving the foreground, showing the keyboard, hardware-key presses or directly touching a follower pauses the session. The optional automatic resume behaviors above handle screen rotation, returning from follower input and a running group's return from the background. A lost connection, unsupported replay API, expired command, invalid contact transition or slow follower also stops forwarding. Pending moves are discarded and any synthetic contact is cancelled. Align the app screens before resuming. Setup can remove selected followers that are no longer shown.

Four games loading or contacting their servers can delay input processing or acknowledgments. A delivery acknowledgment now gets up to three seconds instead of one; start preparation gets five seconds instead of three. The 750 ms input-age limit is unchanged. An acknowledgment for input already delivered while fresh can be accepted later, but pending input is never made fresh or replayed on a later game screen. Setup distinguishes an unacknowledged window (`lc.tapSync.tooSlow`) from discarded late/out-of-order input (`lc.tapSync.expiredInput`). Both still require aligning the screens and explicitly resuming, because a command may have reached only some accounts. The foreground option does not override these failures. Performance CSV version 3 includes `sync_status` to correlate the stated pause reason with recorded load; CPU percentage alone cannot establish guest main-loop responsiveness.

A workspace saves source/target container identifiers and an optional `mode: "singleFinger"` or `mode: "twoFingers"` inside its `tapSync` group. Missing or unknown modes use Taps Only. In a saved workspace's detail page, **Sync Group** edits the group and mode without launching apps or changing the running session. Save patches the latest stored record and preserves its name, layout, ID and library order; clearing and saving removes only the group. Invalid edits are rejected, and deleted records are not recreated. Restoring, duplicating or editing a workspace never enables forwarding or restores contacts/queued input. Invalid imported groups are discarded without losing the layout.

**Performance Monitor** is independent of the sync group and works with a single virtual app window. Its movable overlay samples once per second while enabled and the host view is active; closing it stops sampling. It reports each process's CPU/physical footprint, combined values only with complete coverage, system thermal state, and sync response statistics. See [Performance testing](PerformanceTesting.md) for metric definitions, limitations and repeatable comparisons.

## Implementation and validation

- Each virtual LiveProcess launch receives its own anonymous NSXPC listener endpoint. The host binds it to the launched process identifier, and each connection represents one container; packets cannot select a different source by supplying an identifier.
- Protocol version 5 prepares the mode, return-to-source preference and first-return preference on every participant, with followers ready before the source. The guest observes `UIApplication.sendEvent:` for capture and keeps a weak identity registry of physical contacts even while forwarding is paused. Taps and single-finger input keep their tested packet/replay paths. Two-finger frames carry shared geometry/time and sorted contact identities, phases, normalized coordinates and tap counts. They include every held and released contact; up to four entries allow releasing two old contacts and beginning two new ones in the same frame, with at most two left down. Inactive input sessions perform no capture/replay; there is no input polling, screenshot processing or input saved to disk.
- Source movement uses one-shot coalescing at 60 Hz; a pending final move is flushed before end/cancel. Holds without movement send no keepalives. The host keeps at most four pending packets and may replace only an unsent consecutive move of the same contact. It validates every source sequence and assigns a separate contiguous delivery sequence after coalescing. A full queue causes a pause/cancellation rather than silently losing a contact boundary. UI counts and status do not refresh for every move, and full host geometry scans happen at gesture beginnings and layout changes.
- Two-finger coalescing requires the same contact set and no boundaries. If finger A moved in an older unsent frame but is stationary in the newer frame, its movement phase is retained alongside finger B's newer movement. Missing contacts, reused identities, geometry changes and invalid stationary coordinates reject the whole frame without partially advancing lifecycle state. All contact beginnings/releases/cancellations survive queue coalescing. A completed two-finger gesture is counted only when its final finger ends without a prior cancellation.
- Followers construct a guest-local `UITouch` and a separate `UITouchesEvent`, with digitizer metadata. The same touch persists across all gesture phases and keeps its original hit view, previous position and relative timestamps. Later packets that arrive early wait asynchronously to preserve timing; long presses keep the contact down until release or cancellation. Tap-only replay retains its short asynchronous hold. The system's physical-touch event is never cleared or reused. No global event-dispatch service or new entitlement is used. Private selectors and HID functions are checked before enabling replay; missing APIs and Objective-C exceptions disable it.
- Two-finger replay retains a separate `UITouch` for each identity and sends all contacts in one `UITouchesEvent` with an aggregate digitizer hand. Each touch also has its own HID identity. Delivered-down flags are independent of `UITouch.phase`, so a failed release still attempts cancellation of every delivered contact. Direct physical input excludes all owned synthetic contacts, and delayed frames are bound to the session, sequence and replay generation.
- Every start has a fresh session ID. Host and receivers validate contact transitions, identity, geometry and nondecreasing timestamps. Packets expire after 750 ms; this bounds delivery age, not the length of a stationary hold. Only the current command has a three-second reply-timeout timer, cancelled upon completion or pause. Late replies, delayed moves and scheduled delivery cannot affect a new session.
- Automatic recovery invalidates the old host queue, cancels replay on every guest and waits for all suspension replies and follower fingers to clear. With first-return sync off, source fingers must also clear. Session-bound monotonic revisions prevent old replies from replacing newer touch state; physical begin timestamps order source/follower input across processes. The guest keeps observation alive while suspended but blocks all replay. Once input is ready, one-shot layout checks run at 100 ms intervals for at most three seconds and require 200 ms of unchanged valid geometry. First-return sync can skip this settle delay only when the running group's valid geometry is unchanged. No readiness polling runs while required fingers are down, a source return is missing or acknowledgments are pending. Actual host interface rotation or UIKit's rotation transition identifies a rotation; a rotating device with orientation locked and ordinary resizing do not qualify. Rotation still waits for the transition to finish. A fresh session and participant input checks are required before resuming; counts survive a successful temporary pause, while performance measurements start a new segment. Failed input checks stay paused.
- The new first-return capture lives only in memory, expires 750 ms after its physical begin and is bounded to 64 packets with consecutive move coalescing. A return revision, physical-touch identity allowlist and unchanged guest window/bounds/insets/orientation permit the held source contact through the special source preparation. The host validates the whole capture atomically into a separate bounded queue; the ordinary live continuation retains its four-packet limit. Both the capture and source preparation reply must arrive before any replay starts, in either order. Original timestamps and contact boundaries remain intact, and packets keep the usual expiry checks while draining. Manual pause, stale tokens, new follower input during preparation and changed geometry discard the handover. Time-ordered follower invalidation cannot clear a newer source gesture.
- Foreground recovery binds the intent to the scene that hosted the running session, including while its view is detached. Either a did-enter-background or will-enter-foreground notification supplies background evidence; a did-become-active notification alone does not. App Switcher activation/deactivation before readiness keeps that evidence, while a new interruption after a successful start has to qualify again. Generation checks invalidate earlier activation/layout callbacks on a later departure or cancellation. Readiness is rechecked for at most five seconds during the return, allowing application and scene snapshots to catch up, including an initial background snapshot after this host's own return notification. Another scene activating the process while the host remains backgrounded does not poll or cancel the host's pending intent. Other scenes cannot qualify the host's return or start its input, and attachment to a different scene cancels it.
- Every start requires an active host scene and keeps all visibility/geometry checks. When a visible hosted participant reports foreground inactivity, the host uses the existing scene-reactivation path and retries preparation at 200 ms intervals within the five-second overall preparation deadline. An input window still initializing gets the same bounded wait. First-return handover retains its shorter retry count and 750 ms capture-age limit. Keyboard, physical-touch, covering-window and unsupported-API failures are not retried or bypassed. The foreground option first allows up to five seconds for a valid layout stable for 200 ms, then prepares followers before the source; it retains no old payload and has no periodic work while backgrounded or ordinarily idle.
- Guest obstruction checks first select the normal content window, then inspect higher windows in its own scene. Empty transparent structural windows and windows whose hit-test passes input through no longer count as covering the game merely because their level is higher. UIKit's automatically installed private window recognizers for focus and pointer infrastructure, and its single-target keyboard-dismissal tap, do not count as app content; other public and app-defined window recognizers still do. Intersecting visible content, enabled controls/gestures or custom drawing/touch receivers that take input still block, as do visible alerts presented in the content window. All rectangles are converted to guest coordinates. Window selection remains conservative when content is ambiguous. UIKit simulator regressions cover these cases; the reported immediate false pause on iPad Pro M1 running iOS 27.0.1 still needs an on-device retest.
- Last-selection persistence accepts only bounded container names, deduplicates followers and excludes the source. A malformed follower list is discarded, unknown modes use Taps Only, and old mode-only preferences migrate. No running-window filter is applied while loading, so opening accounts one at a time does not erase the saved group. Identifiers, mode and the three automatic-resume preferences are written to defaults; session IDs, queued packets, contacts, pending recovery and active state remain in memory.
- Guest input connections attempt three bounded reconnects after disconnection. Returning to the foreground or receiving a valid preparation request resets the retry budget; idle connected sessions do not poll. Transport recovery leaves input paused. Old connection callbacks and requests cannot clear a replacement connection or its session. Deferred keyboard/foreground interruptions are bound to the session generation at the event that caused them. Foreign keyboard notifications do not latch the local keyboard flag; hide/background notifications clear it.
- Synthetic-touch ownership uses weak object identity and survives session cleanup. A replay contact retained by UIKit cannot be mistaken for physical input after pause/restart, while real touches remain blockers. The registry does not retain old touches or their views. Missing replay APIs and failed cancellation still retain the existing unsupported-state safeguards.
- Recovery regressions cover all-account cancellation acknowledgments, source-return and finger-release barriers, old/reordered snapshots and input, cross-account begin ordering, rotation-to-follower interruption, stable geometry and cancelled recovery. Client-state tests cover multiple equal-valued physical touch identities, release, weak lifetime and held fingers across session replacement. Return-buffer and host-session tests cover complete taps, move coalescing, atomic two-finger boundaries, bounded capture, expiry, all-or-nothing invalid capture rejection and ordered live continuation. The iPad simulator harness exercises the production coordinator with four peer accounts for whole-group suspension, fresh-session resume, preserved counts, ignored old input/replies, waiting for other fingers, manual pause, rotation transitions, ordinary resize, keyboard/background/disconnection and remembered preferences. It also verifies immediate first-return preparation, held-source recovery, completed taps during preparation, payload/reply ordering and cancellation during handover. Guest regressions exercise local option-off returns, the first returned tap and held single/two-finger continuation, stale/unrelated-contact rejection, cancelling replay for a real follower touch and ignoring owned synthetic contacts/other-window return signals. The peer/input and transition signals and synthetic cancellation delivery in these tests are controlled; real game touch injection and physical-device rotation still need device testing.
- Automated tests cover mapping, outside tracking, invalid numbers, orientation/aspect changes, contact lifetimes, long holds, cancellation, malformed packets, ordering, atomic frames, coalescing across both fingers, queue pressure, expiry, stopped sessions, saved group/mode editing with stale readers and bounded performance statistics. CI compiles and packages the host and guest. The user verified tap-only sync with two/four instances and reported no major issues in the subsequent single-finger/control build `196fe7e`. Two-finger replay, the new overlay and all latency/thermal conclusions still require physical-device checks; build success is not device validation.

## Removing the Experimental label

Load and foreground regressions in the iPad simulator additionally cover acknowledgment after one second, an outstanding window at the three-second limit, discarded stale queues, startup after three seconds and initializing-window retries. Foreground tests cover default off, fresh-session recovery with no extra touch, old reply/packet rejection, manual pause/group/option/mode edits, pending startup/follower pause, stable layout and every fresh participant check, dialogs/keyboards/physical touch/disconnection, repeated lifecycle signals and temporary inactivity. Home/App Switcher regressions exercise foreground notifications with no background note, a temporarily detached host view, activation before the public scene snapshot or return evidence, an interrupted return and unrelated scene notifications. These controlled sequences address lifecycle gaps; they do not establish the exact notification order on iPad Pro M1 / iOS 27.0.1.

Input Sync remains experimental until its supported device/app combinations have repeatable physical-device evidence. For the iPad Pro M1 / iOS 27.0.1 and intended game, run the device checklist with two and four accounts in every input mode, both first-return settings, source/follower switches and portrait/both landscape directions. Each combination must complete at least 50 return/resume cycles and a 30-minute normal play session without missed or duplicate actions, stuck contacts, unexplained pauses or replay of old input. Include manual pause, real dialogs/keyboards, background/lock with the foreground option both off and on, rotation while holding fingers and an unresponsive follower, then verify recovery or the stated pause reason. Also record four-game startup and post-stage server requests with CSV `sync_status`; distinguish actual packet expiry from missing acknowledgment. Relaunch must restore the choices paused. Record the game/device versions and compare idle and active overhead. A failed case stays open and is retested after its fix; CI, package inspection and a forwarding counter alone do not satisfy these criteria. Removing the label would apply to the verified compatibility scope, without claiming every game or iOS build is supported.

See the device checklist in `Tests/MultitaskManualChecks.md` before treating this as a stable feature.
